The American Academy of Pediatrics advises that children under 18 months avoid screen use other than video chatting, and that those between 2 and 5 years old be limited to one hour per day of high-quality programming. These guidelines reflect a cautious stance grounded in concerns about developmental impact during early childhood, though the strength of evidence underlying specific thresholds varies, and research in this area continues to evolve.

A notable tension exists between these recommendations and the way screen-based products are frequently presented to parents. Manufacturers of devices and apps often market their products as educational or developmentally beneficial for young children, yet independent evidence substantiating many of these claims remains limited. Parents should approach such marketing with skepticism, recognizing that commercial interests do not always align with what the research supports.
